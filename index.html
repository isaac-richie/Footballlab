How it works

How we work out the chances

No pundit's hunch and no gut feeling. Fairline turns past results and bookmaker prices into the chance of every outcome, then puts its picks on a public record so you can judge it on results.

The methodThe proofReading the numbersWhat we can't know

01From results to a percentage

  1. Collect the data. Every result for each league going back several seasons, with half-time scores where they exist, plus bookmaker prices posted before kickoff. The data is checked for bad scores and duplicates on every refresh.
  2. Rate every team. Each side gets an attack rating and a defence rating, learned only from games already played. Recent matches count most, so a team's rating moves as its form does.
  3. Build every possible scoreline. The ratings give each team's expected goals, which become a grid of every score from 0–0 upwards with its chance. Win, draw, goals, both teams to score, handicaps, half-time and combos are all read off that same grid, so they always agree with each other.
  4. Start from the sharpest price. Where bookmakers have posted odds, we strip out their cut and build the grid from their prices instead, because in testing they were a little sharper than our model alone. Where there are no prices, the model stands on its own.

02The proof

We test the model the hard way: it predicts games it has never seen, using only what was known before kickoff, and we count how often it was right.

Being right more often is only half of it. The other half is being honest about how sure we are: when we say 70%, it should happen about 7 times in 10, not 9. That is what our record tracks, losses included.

03Reading the numbers

A percentage
How often we expect it to happen. 60% means about 6 times in 10, so it also fails about 4 times in 10.
Fair odds
The price that matches our chance with no bookmaker cut: 1 divided by the chance. 50% is fair odds of 2.00; 25% is 4.00.
Value
When your bookie pays more than our fair odds, the price is in your favour. It is still not a sure thing; it means the bet is priced better than its chance.
Over 2.5 goals
Three or more goals in the match. Under 2.5 is two or fewer.
Both teams score
Each side scores at least once, whatever the result.
Expected goals
The average number of goals a team would score if the game were played many times. Not shot-based xG.
Worked example

We give Chelsea a 50% chance to win, so their fair odds are 2.00. Your bookie offers 2.20. That price pays out as if Chelsea win about 45% of the time, below our 50%, so it is value. At 1.80 it would not be.

04What we can't know

  • Team news. We don't see lineups, injuries, suspensions or rotation. If a star is out, the bookie's price will move before ours does.
  • Every pick can lose. An 80% pick still loses about 1 time in 5. Combining picks in one bet multiplies those risks.
  • Bookies are sharp. In testing, their prices were slightly more accurate than our model alone, which is why we start from them. Beating them over time is hard.
  • The past isn't a promise. Every figure here comes from games already played. Leagues change, and so can how well the model does.
